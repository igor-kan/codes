# Demonstrating Ownership

Ownership means you act like the long-term owner of the problem, not a
temporary caretaker.

## Behaviors

- Notice problems outside your immediate scope and fix or escalate them.
- Follow incidents to root cause and drive the prevention work.
- Track your systems with metrics, alerts, and runbooks.
- Reduce toil through automation instead of heroics.
- Mentor others and improve team processes.

## Strong story pattern

> I noticed [problem] that was nobody's explicit job.
> I owned [diagnosis/design/delivery], coordinated [people], and shipped
> [change]. Result: [metric]. I then [prevented recurrence / documented /
> generalized], so it stayed fixed.

## Metrics that read well

- Incident frequency/MTTR reduction.
- Cost savings or capacity reclaimed.
- Onboarding time, test coverage, deploy frequency.
- Customer-visible reliability or performance.

## Prompts

- "Tell me about a time you took on something outside your responsibilities."
- "How have you improved the systems you own?"
